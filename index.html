Where MobilityWare Wins Next

A strategic perspective on UA in the privacy-first, LTV-first era.

Prepared for Chris Kim and Corvin Ampleev, Directors of User Acquisition
By Qirui Zheng

0Why this memo

Most candidates submit a résumé and hope the conversation does the rest. I'd rather show you, on paper, how I think about the business you actually run — so when we talk, we're working from a shared frame instead of building one from scratch.

This is a single-pass strategic read. It's organized around six questions a UA Director thinks about: where the industry is going, where MobilityWare sits inside it, who's beating us and how, what hurts most, what I'd do about it, and what the first ninety days look like. Where I'm inferring rather than knowing, I say so.

That growth is no longer evenly distributed. Three structural shifts have re-shaped UA economics in the last 24 months:

1.1 The post-ATT measurement world is permanent

SKAN postbacks arrive 24–72 hours late and at coarsened granularity. iOS opt-in rates for casual gaming sit around 30–38%. The "we'll wait for things to normalize" posture has run out of runway — partners and tools that don't operate cleanly under SKAN are not coming back.

1.2 High-value users dominate revenue concentration

Per Moloco's 2025 State of Mobile Gaming, the top 5% of US iOS players (just 0.02% of global installs) generate 20% of global gaming revenue. Top 1% iOS bid prices rose 140% in 2024. The competitive auction is no longer about installs at scale — it's about installs that compound.

1.3 Casual card has bifurcated

Classic mechanics + ad monetization (the historical MobilityWare lane) now competes with meta-game-driven IAP studios (Playtika's Solitaire Grand Harvest) and real-money skill operators (Papaya's Solitaire Cash). The economic envelopes are no longer comparable.

Why these compound Privacy constrains measurement → which makes high-value-user identification harder → which makes meta-game-driven LTV more strategically valuable → which raises the CPI ceiling competitors can sustain. A UA team that doesn't read these forces together will lose ground at every step.

The portfolio — Solitaire, Spider Solitaire, FreeCell, Pyramid, TriPeaks, Castle, Mahjong, Bubble Shooter Pop, plus licensed titles like Monopoly Solitaire — represents 35 years of category authority that no challenger can replicate. The audience is loyal, lightweight, and offline-friendly in ways that survive every platform shift.

Structural Vulnerability The portfolio is predominantly ad-monetized. In a privacy-first world, ad-monetized titles face two compounding pressures simultaneously:
  • Their measurement layer is the most degraded by ATT.
  • Their LTV ceiling is set by ad-revenue rates rather than IAP depth.

The classic + daily-challenge gameplay loop has aged — recent additions (Jigsaw events, Arcade Goal, Winning Animation Collection, weekly tournaments, multiplayer) are the right direction but trail full meta-game competitors by a meaningful gap.

This is not a critique. It's the work that's in front of the UA team — and the reason a thoughtful analyst hire matters now.

3Who's beating us, and how

Five competitors define the strategic landscape. Each plays a different game.

Structural Threat

Solitaire Grand Harvest (Playtika / Supertreat)

Married TriPeaks mechanics to a farm meta-game that lifted retention, IAP penetration, and LTV simultaneously. Public estimates suggest 50M+ installs and IAP shares around 85%.

Different Rules

Solitaire Cash (Papaya) & Solitaire Smash (Playtika)

Real-money gaming (RMG), age-gated, geo-restricted, with revenue per active user 5–7× a typical free-to-play casual title.

What we can borrow: creative hooks (urgency, leaderboard, near-loss recovery) and D7 retention tactics.

What we cannot borrow: their unit economics. Competitive decks that pretend RMG is comparable to F2P are wrong.

Brand & Distribution

Microsoft Solitaire Collection

Low UA spend (estimated under $1M monthly), heavy organic share via Xbox Live, daily-challenge cadence as a retention engine.

The lesson: ASO and store-listing CRO can substitute for paid spend at the right brand level.

Quality Benchmark

Brainium

Clean classic gameplay, strong reviews, light ads + IAP. Worth watching as the control group for "what does a high-quality classic experience without a meta-layer look like at scale."

Operational Threat

Tripledot Studios

Classic mechanics with light meta, but a UA-first organizational culture and a creative iteration cadence (weekly) that's 2× faster than most studios.

Reframe We're not behind a different company — we're a parallel company with different leverage. They have UA capability discipline. We have OG brand authority and 100M+ installs of cross-promo inventory most studios would kill to own.

4What hurts most ●

Four pain points, in priority order. (Click each tab — these are the difficulties that frame the initiatives in §5.)

Pain 4.1 — Attribution Fragmentation UA data lives in 10+ silos that disagree by double digits. Network self-report claims more than MMP credits more than SKAN postbacks more than incrementality measures. A single campaign can produce four different "true" install counts depending on which dashboard you open.

This isn't a tooling problem — AppsFlyer SSOT (which the team already runs, per the InMobi case study) is the right backbone. It's a trust framework problem: which partners deserve which level of reported-vs-incremental discount, and how does that show up in budget allocation. Until that framework is codified, every channel-mix decision is partly negotiation, partly guesswork.

My Solution → Initiative 5.1 A per-partner trust score across 5 dimensions, applied as a discount on reported ROAS. Turns "Meta feels overstated" into a number that travels to Finance.
Pain 4.2 — Meta-game LTV Ceiling As §3 framed, the Grand Harvest gap is roughly 0.6–0.8× in pLTV/CPI terms. This is the single largest structural variable in MobilityWare's UA economics.

It's not a UA team problem — it's a product roadmap input that the UA team should advocate for with data. Recent product moves (Jigsaw events, Arcade Goal) are pointing the right direction; the question is whether UA can quantify which meta-features earn the LTV needed to support competitive CPI bids on iOS.

My Solution → Initiative 5.2 Whale-lookalike loop on first-party HVU signature. Buys time while product closes the meta-game gap by extracting the LTV we already have.
Pain 4.3 — SKAN-aware Creative Cadence Chris's split of UA and creative as "two separate tracks for the brain" was the right organizational call. The follow-on operational challenge is making creative testing produce reliable readouts when SKAN conversion values are coarse and probabilistic data is sparse.

Most studios still test creative the way they tested it in 2020 and accept the noise. The opportunity is a CV-mapping + 2-week cadence designed for SKAN, not despite it.

My Solution → Initiative 5.3 6-bucket conversion-value mapping, restricted to networks with creative-ID propagation 4+/5, validated by Spearman ρ > 0.6 against Android determined-data rankings.
Pain 4.4 — Emerging-markets Under-investment Tier-1 markets generate 35% of global mobile gaming revenue but show slowing growth from saturation. Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia, the Philippines have lower bid pressure, growing iOS share, and D7 retention parity to T1 in casual card.

The honest read on most studios' emerging-market efforts: pilots fail by being half-funded. Commit fully or don't run.

My Solution → Initiative 5.4 $180K, 6-week sprint in BR + MX (iOS) and ID + PH (Android), with day-one geo-holdout incrementality. Reserve budget = zero.

5What I'd do — five initiatives ●

Each ladders to one or more of the pain points above. I'd pitch these for execution in order of the leverage-to-effort ratio.

5.1Build the trust-framework layer on top of the SSOT spine

Maps to pain: 4.1 Attribution fragmentation

Mechanic
Codify a per-partner trust score across 5 dimensions:
  1. MMP-to-network reconciliation rate
  2. SKAN-to-probabilistic ratio (Chris's red-flag metric)
  3. Postback completeness
  4. Creative-ID propagation
  5. Cohort joinability
Score every partner 1–5 on each. Apply trust-weighted discounts to reported ROAS in the channel-performance scorecard.
Output
Budget reallocation conversations stop being "Meta self-reports 35% D7 ROAS, that looks fine" and start being "Meta MMP ROAS is 21%, applied incrementality discount of 0.41 lands true incremental ROAS at roughly 8.5%, recommended -50% spend pending partner conversation."
Effort
Per-network scorecard refresh weekly + alert when any dimension drops a level. First version shippable in 30 days from existing AppsFlyer SSOT.
Why it matters This is the artifact that turns a Director's instinct ("Meta feels overstated") into a number that travels to Finance without translation loss.

5.2The high-value-user lookalike loop

Maps to pain: 4.2 Meta-game LTV ceiling

Mechanic
Identify the top 5% of spenders across the portfolio, extract their first-party feature set (early-event signature, channel of acquisition, geo, device, pre-IAP behavioral signals), seed lookalike audiences on Meta CPP, Moloco custom audiences, ASA Custom Product Pages, and TikTok. Run as a 50/50 split against standard prospecting on a 6-week test budget.
Why us
Ad-monetized portfolios don't usually invest in lookalike infrastructure because IAP signals are weaker. But our long retention curves give us a different signal — early-day session depth and ad-engagement frequency are predictive of long-term ad revenue contribution. We have first-party signal that competitors don't bother building.
Expected
Higher CPI (+20–30%), but D30 ROAS ≥ 60% and 3–4× longer retention on the lookalike cohort. The economics work because the high-value tail compounds.

5.3SKAN-optimized creative iteration engine

Maps to pain: 4.3 SKAN-aware creative cadence

Mechanic
A 2-week creative testing cycle with conversion-value mapping designed for SKAN — 6 buckets covering:
  • D0 install
  • D0 first session
  • D0 second session
  • D0 paywall view
  • D0 IAP
  • D0 ad-revenue threshold
Run only on networks where creative-ID propagation scores 4+/5 in the trust framework (effectively: ASA, Meta, AppLovin, Moloco).
Validation
A meta-test: Spearman ρ > 0.6 between SKAN-readout rankings and Android determined-data rankings. If they correlate, the SKAN readouts are real.
Why this fits Chris's org design Creative gets a clean testing surface; UA gets a scoring framework that doesn't lie under privacy constraints.

5.4Emerging-markets sprint with day-one geo holdouts

Maps to pain: 4.4 Emerging-markets under-investment

Mechanic
6-week pilot in Brazil and Mexico (iOS) + Indonesia and the Philippines (Android). $180K test budget. Geo-holdout incrementality from day one — non-negotiable for a new-geo expansion, because without it we won't be able to distinguish "TikTok BR is genuinely cheap" from "we got lucky on a creative."
Localization
Localized creative hooks per market:
  • "jogo offline" in Portuguese for BR's mobile-data-cost reality
  • "jugar con la familia" in Spanish for MX's family-occasion gaming context
Hard truth Emerging-markets pilots fail by being half-funded. The reserve budget on this initiative should be zero — commit fully or don't run.

5.5Cross-title portfolio synergy as paid-equivalent inventory

Maps to pain: 4.1 + structural under-utilization

Mechanic
Formalize cross-promotion between Solitaire ↔ Spider ↔ TriPeaks ↔ Bubble Shooter Pop. Industry benchmark for cross-promo as a share of UA mix in multi-title studios is 12–18%. MobilityWare's current rate is plausibly closer to 3–4% based on the lack of public emphasis.
The hidden asset We're sitting on the largest piece of paid-equivalent UA inventory in the portfolio and barely using it.
Tradeoff
Every Solitaire ad slot we use for cross-promotion is third-party ad revenue we forgo. At MobilityWare's spend tier, that's likely $150K/quarter. The trade is worth it if cross-promoted users have D30 LTV at parity to paid acquisition users — which is the readable test design.
Loop back
The infrastructure question (how do we attribute a Solitaire-acquired user who installs TriPeaks 14 days later) is itself a fragmentation problem, which loops back to Initiative 5.1.

6The first ninety days

If I'm hired, the first 90 days look like this.

Days 1–30

Listen and instrument

  • Ride along with each UA manager across all 4-ish active titles
  • Audit existing dashboards; produce written report on duplication, gaps, trust
  • Land v1 of a daily reconciliation report (feeds Initiative 5.1) — Slack-delivered every weekday at 8am PT
  • Output @ week 4: one-page diagnostic memo for both of you
Days 31–60

Ship reusable infrastructure

  • Per-network trust scorecard (refresh weekly)
  • Channel-performance daily scorecard (Tab 3 of the workflow)
  • Creative-fatigue early-warning report
  • Scope first incrementality test on the largest channel — likely Google UAC at portfolio scale
Days 61–90

Move a metric

  • Run and read out the first incrementality test
  • Co-author the first creative testing brief with the Creative team using the SKAN-aware framework
  • Propose Initiative 5.4 (BR iOS expansion sprint) for execution
  • Lands a concrete first deliverable on Corvin's market-expansion theme
What I'm NOT proposing for the first 90 days Initiative 5.2 (whale lookalikes) depends on first-party data plumbing that may not exist on day one. Initiative 5.3 (SKAN creative engine) depends on the trust framework being live first. Both wait until quarter two.

7A closing note

The work in front of MobilityWare is the kind of work that genuinely interests me: data that's messy by structural necessity, real money on the line, decisions that compound over months rather than days, and a transformation arc — from "buy installs cheaply, monetize via ads" to "identify high-LTV users, retain them with live ops, attribute correctly under privacy constraints" — that's already underway and needs analytical horsepower to finish.

Chris, your read on the post-ATT landscape and the UA-creative split is the operational frame I'd want to work inside. Corvin, your long-term-value framing and the high-value-user thesis is the strategic frame I'd want to point analytical work toward. Together they're the two halves of the same shift, and the gap between them is exactly where a thoughtful analyst hire creates leverage.

I'd be glad to walk through any of this in detail.
